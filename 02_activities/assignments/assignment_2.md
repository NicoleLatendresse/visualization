## Good Vidualization
https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/serena.purslow/viz/ThePriceofProsperity-C02emissionsGDPandPopulationtrendsGlobally/PriceofProsperity
      
![Good Visualization](<Price of Prosperity.png>)
    
### What makes this a good visualization?

**Aesthetic: Good use of colour and placement**
The colours in this visualization are consistent from one visualization to the next, making it easy to move from one visualization to the next and understand what's going on. The charts are also well positioned, there's a clear flow from the visualization title and description, to the world map, then breaking down by country. 

**Perceptual: Clear message**
Each of the visualizations contribute toward a clear message, with each building on each other. The description on the left side is clear and concise, only giving you the information you need to understand the message. Each chart is clearly labelled, with subtitles to tell you which parts are interactive. 

**Substantive: Honest reflection of the data**
Although it is clear that the visualization has a clear takeaway, that CO2 emission is tied to GDP and population, the visualization is not biased in how it displays the data. It displays all data and allows us to see and interpret the trends that emerge in the data. 

### How could this data visualization be improved?

1. Colour choice: while there is colour consistency that allows you to easily distinguish between each country, the light colours are harder to see and differentiate between. While this may look pretty, it isn't the most accessible choice of colours. I would have preferred to see a darker colour scheme.

2. Scale: to make the individual country charts easier to compare with each other, I would keep the scale the same between each of the countries. If you don't immediately notice the difference in scale, it may cause you to misinterpret two countries having the same CO2 emissions and/or GDP, even though they are quite different. However, this may also make some countries, like Africa harder to visualize, as it would compress the results to one corner. So there are pros and cons to this one. 



## Bad Visualization
https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/spec.bi/viz/HealthInsuranceDashboard_15943803590210/HealthInsuranceAnalysis

![Bad Visualization](<Health Insurance Analysis.png>)

### What makes this a bad visualization?

**Not Aesthetic: No colour continuity and no thought to placement**
The visualization uses multiple different colour schemes, making it difficult to follow how one chart relates to the next. The labels for each chart are in a light grey, which don't make them start out, so you struggle to find the point of each of them. All other text, like axes labels, are too small to have any impact. Lastly, there is no thought given to the placement and size of each of the charts. It's not clear which one you should be looking at first and how one ties to the next. It seems like a whole bunch of different charts haphazardly thrown together. 

**Not Perceptual: No clear message**
There is no clear message to the visualization. It feels like it's just trying to throw a bunch of different information at you in one place. You get a different message with each chart you look at. The title of "Insurance Analysis" also doesn't tell us much.

**Substantive But Not Meaningful**
The table is substantive because it is honest and unbiased, but in an attempt to display all the available data, it lacks a meaningful message and has no clear target audience. 


### How could this data visualization be improved?

1. Cut down charts and decide on a message: The visualization needs to be more clear on what message it's trying to convey, and by narrowing down that objective, it will also help to decide which charts are important to keep and which ones can be cut out. I think this could even be split into two different visualizations. It's usually not a good idea to try to cram as much information as possible into one visualization. 

2. Colour choice: Once you have one clear message, it will be easier to create a consistent colour scheme, as it's likely that you'll be using similar concepts across each of your charts. 

3. Arrangement and size: using different sizes and arrangements can help make your message clearer. Right now, all charts are very similar in size, but making some of the more important charts bigger and closer to the upper right (where we typically look first) can help to draw out attention to them and identify them as being important.
